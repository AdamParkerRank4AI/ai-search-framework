# Best Places to Go Magnet Fishing in Colchester

Colchester, Britain's oldest recorded town, sits along the River Colne and has a rich history stretching back to Roman times. Its ancient waterways, historic bridges and former port areas make it an excellent destination for magnet fishing enthusiasts. This guide covers the best spots to try, what you might find and the rules you need to follow.

---

## Top Magnet Fishing Spots in Colchester

### 1. The Hythe

The Hythe is Colchester's historic port area and one of the most promising magnet fishing locations in the city. It has been in use since Roman times and served as a busy trading port for centuries. By 1892, approximately 3,000 vessels were using the port each year, mainly Thames barges along with small coasting vessels and cargo steamers. Centuries of maritime activity mean the riverbed around the Hythe is likely to hold a wide variety of metal objects, from old mooring hardware and tools to coins and historical artefacts.

**What to expect:** Maritime hardware, old tools, coins, nails and potentially items dating back several centuries.

**Access:** The Hythe is accessible via Hythe Quay, with public footpaths along the waterfront.

### 2. East Bridge and East Mill Area

East Bridge marks the tidal limit of the River Colne in Colchester. This area has been a crossing point for centuries, and bridges are consistently ranked among the best locations for magnet fishing anywhere in the UK. Items are frequently dropped, thrown or lost from bridges, and the slower water flow near tidal limits means objects tend to accumulate rather than wash downstream.

**What to expect:** Coins, keys, tools, bicycle parts and general metalwork from centuries of use as a river crossing.

**Access:** Located in central Colchester, easily accessible on foot.

### 3. Middleborough and North Bridge

The area around North Bridge where the River Colne passes through central Colchester is another strong option. As a key crossing point in one of England's oldest towns, this bridge and the surrounding riverbanks have seen continuous human activity for well over a thousand years. The Roman wall ran close to the river here, adding to the historical potential.

**What to expect:** A mix of modern and historical finds. The Roman heritage of the area adds the small but exciting possibility of ancient metalwork.

**Access:** Central Colchester, public access along the river path.

### 4. River Colne Upstream — Lexden and West Bergholt

Further upstream from the town centre, the River Colne passes through quieter, more rural areas around Lexden and West Bergholt. Smaller bridges and crossing points along this stretch can be productive, as they receive less attention from other magnet fishers. The slower, shallower water also makes retrieval easier.

**What to expect:** Agricultural tools, horseshoes, old fencing materials and general rural metalwork.

**Access:** Footpaths and country lanes provide access to various points along the river. Always check you are not on private land.

### 5. Colne Causeway and Sheepen Road Area

The Colne Causeway area, near the site of the ancient Sheepen settlement (a major pre-Roman and Roman industrial site), is historically significant. Archaeological excavations in this area have revealed extensive evidence of metalworking. While major artefacts have been recovered by archaeologists, the waterway here may still hold interesting finds.

**What to expect:** A mix of historical and modern items. The archaeological significance of the area adds interest, though significant finds should be reported.

**Access:** Accessible from Sheepen Road and surrounding footpaths.

## What You Might Find

Colchester's long history means magnet fishing here can turn up a wide range of items:

- **Modern items:** Coins, keys, tools, bicycle parts, phones, fishing tackle
- **Maritime artefacts:** Mooring chains, anchors, boat fittings (especially near the Hythe)
- **Historical metalwork:** Old nails, horseshoes, buckles, buttons and agricultural tools
- **Military items:** Colchester has a long military history, including a prominent garrison, so military-related finds are possible

## Rules and Legal Considerations

Before heading out, be aware of the following:

### Permission

- **There is no specific law banning magnet fishing in England**, but you must have permission from the landowner or managing body of the waterway.
- **The Canal & River Trust does not permit magnet fishing** on waterways it manages. Check whether your chosen spot falls under Canal & River Trust jurisdiction.
- For the River Colne in Colchester, sections may be managed by the Environment Agency or local authorities. Contact the relevant body before fishing.

### Safety

- **Never magnet fish alone.** Waterways can be dangerous.
- **Wear gloves.** Retrieved items may be sharp, corroded or contaminated.
- **Report any weapons or suspected explosives immediately** to the police by calling 999. Do not attempt to move them.
- **Dispose of rubbish responsibly.** If you pull up scrap metal or litter, take it with you and dispose of it properly.

### Archaeological Finds

- Under the **Treasure Act 1996**, certain finds (particularly items over 300 years old containing precious metal) must be reported to the local coroner within 14 days.
- If you find something that looks historically significant, contact Colchester Museum or the local Finds Liaison Officer.
- Colchester has **Scheduled Monument** areas. Magnet fishing on or near scheduled monuments without consent from Historic England is a criminal offence.

## Equipment Recommendations

For the River Colne in Colchester, consider:

- **Magnet strength:** A single-sided neodymium magnet with at least 200kg (440lb) pull force is suitable for most spots. The Hythe area, with deeper water, may benefit from a stronger magnet.
- **Rope:** At least 20 metres of strong, non-stretch rope (paracord or dedicated magnet fishing rope).
- **Gloves:** Cut-resistant gloves are essential.
- **Bucket or bag:** For carrying your finds and any rubbish you retrieve.
- **Grappling hook:** Useful for snagging larger items that the magnet alone cannot lift.

---

## Tips for Success

1. **Focus on bridges and crossing points.** These are historically the most productive spots for magnet fishing anywhere.
2. **Try multiple throws from different angles.** Cover as much of the riverbed as possible.
3. **Visit after heavy rain.** Higher water levels can shift sediment and expose previously buried items.
4. **Check both sides of a bridge.** Upstream and downstream sides can yield different results.
5. **Be patient.** The best finds often come after extended sessions in a single spot.
