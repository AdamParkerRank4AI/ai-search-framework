## Executive Summary

When a homebuyer asks ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Perplexity "Who is the best estate agent in [town] for [property type]?", how many agents actually appear in the answer? The emerging evidence suggests: very few. This research brief outlines a study proposal and supporting data that demonstrates the scale of the AI visibility gap in the UK estate agency sector, and why the agents who act now will dominate their local markets for years to come.

---

## The Problem: AI Is Coming, But Almost Nobody Is Ready

The data paints a stark picture. Buyers are already using AI to research property decisions, but estate agents are almost entirely invisible to these platforms.

### Consumer Demand Is Already Here

- **82% of Americans** are using AI for housing market information (Realtor.com, October 2025, n=1,000).
- **ChatGPT (67%)** and **Gemini (54%)** are the most used AI platforms for property research.
- **22% of UK estate agents** report buyers mentioning AI when explaining how they found a property (GetAgent/Find Out Now, February 2026, n=622).

### But Agent Visibility Is Near Zero

- Only **7% of UK estate agents** say AI-powered search tools are currently improving the visibility of their property listings.
- **93% see no impact** from AI search on their business today.
- AI traffic to estate agent websites currently represents just **0.2% of total visits** — but this reflects **1,784% year-on-year growth** (Nurtur, 2025).

### The Industry Knows What Is Coming

- **84% of agents** believe AI-powered search tools will become a primary way buyers search for property.
- **88% of agents** believe businesses that fail to adopt AI search risk falling behind competitors.
- **52% of agents** plan to adopt AI tools for listings, lead generation and marketing in the next 12 months (Alto 2026 Agency Trends Report, n=250).

### The Trajectory Is Exponential

AI traffic to estate agent websites is projected to grow from **0.05% (early 2025)** to potentially **over 40% by mid-2028** (Nurtur). This is not a gradual shift. It is a cliff edge for agents who are not visible.

## Proposed Study: "The AI Recommendation Gap in UK Estate Agency"

### Research Question

When consumers ask AI platforms to recommend estate agents for specific locations and property types across the UK, how many agents are actually named? What determines which agents appear? And what can the 93% who are currently invisible do about it?

### Methodology

#### Phase 1: AI Platform Testing (Quantitative)

Run a structured set of prompts across six major AI platforms:

- ChatGPT (GPT-4o)
- Google Gemini
- Claude
- Perplexity
- Microsoft Copilot
- Google AI Overviews

**Prompt categories:**

| Intent Type | Example Prompt |
|---|---|
| Exploratory | "Who are the best estate agents in [town]?" |
| Transactional | "I need to sell a 3-bed semi in [town], which agent should I use?" |
| Comparative | "Compare estate agents in [town] for selling a family home" |
| Specialist | "Which estate agents in [county] specialise in country estates?" |
| Review-based | "Which estate agents in [town] have the best reviews?" |

**Sample:**
- 50 UK towns and cities (mix of major cities, county towns, and smaller market towns)
- 5 prompt types per location = 250 unique prompts
- 6 AI platforms = 1,500 total queries
- Run each prompt 3 times to test consistency = 4,500 data points

**Data captured per query:**
- Number of agents named
- Specific agents named
- Whether a source/citation was provided
- Whether the agent has structured data on their website
- Whether the agent appears on key third-party platforms
- Consistency across repeated prompts (same agent named each time?)

#### Phase 2: Agent Profile Analysis (Qualitative)

For agents that ARE recommended by AI platforms, analyse their digital presence across the Rank4AI Five Signal Model:

1. **Identity Clarity** — Is the agent clearly categorised? Consistent naming across platforms?
2. **Subject Authority** — Do they have in-depth local area guides, buyer/seller content, market reports?
3. **Meaning Architecture** — Schema markup, structured data, RAG-ready content passages?
4. **Ecosystem Validation** — Presence on Google Business Profile, Trustpilot, Rightmove, Zoopla, industry directories?
5. **Signal Consistency** — Consistent descriptions, categories and terminology across all surfaces?

For agents that are NOT recommended, identify the specific signal gaps.

#### Phase 3: Gap Analysis and Scoring

Score each agent on AI readiness using the Rank4AI AI Visibility Score methodology. Produce a league table of AI-visible vs AI-invisible agents per location.

## Expected Findings (Hypothesis)

Based on the existing data and preliminary testing:

1. **AI platforms will name between 2 and 5 agents per location** — out of dozens or hundreds of active agents. The vast majority will be completely invisible.
2. **Consistency will be low** — the same prompt run three times may produce different agent names, indicating weak signal strength across the board.
3. **National brands and portal-listed agents will dominate** — agents with strong third-party presence (Rightmove profiles, Trustpilot reviews, Google Business Profiles) will appear more frequently.
4. **Independent agents with strong local content will outperform** some larger chains — where they have invested in depth content, local area guides and structured data.
5. **Schema markup and structured data will be a key differentiator** — agents with LocalBusiness, RealEstateAgent and Review schema will be cited more frequently.
6. **The gap between visible and invisible agents will be enormous** — creating a clear first-mover advantage for those who act now.

## Why This Matters: The Compounding Effect

AI platforms build confidence over time. An agent that is consistently recommended today will be harder to displace tomorrow. Every month of inaction increases the gap.

This is not a ranking that resets. It is a trust graph that compounds.

The GetAgent research confirms the industry knows this: 84% of agents believe AI search will become a primary discovery channel, yet 93% see no current impact. This is the definition of a market about to tip. The agents who build their AI visibility foundations now — while competitors are still waiting — will lock in a structural advantage that becomes increasingly difficult to overcome.
