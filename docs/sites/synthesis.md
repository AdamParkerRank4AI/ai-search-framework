## 1. The cross-site picture

Three sites, three jobs, one funnel.

- **BBL** is the trust / citation layer. UK business loans editorial
  only — listicles, head-to-heads, lender reviews, "what is" pillars.
  No forms. Wins AI Overviews and ChatGPT/Perplexity citations on
  "best [loan] for [persona/industry]" and single-lender review
  queries. Out-cites NerdWallet UK, money.co.uk, ExpertSure with a
  dated, methodology-led, named-author programme.
- **FundBiz** is the conversion layer for finance. Application forms,
  eligibility checkers, calculators, sector landers, the
  diagnose-the-decline matcher. Owns post-decline (CCJ, bad credit,
  missed payments, first-year-trading) where no UK competitor runs an
  interactive matcher. Licensed to flex 20% into adjacent SMB finance
  (cards, accounts, MCA, asset finance, VAT loans) when the buyer is
  on a loans journey.
- **CardMachines** is the payments site. Editorial + transactional
  combined. Owns terminal model reviews, "card machine for [trade]"
  landers, switching content (Worldpay/Barclaycard/Elavon exit),
  Tap to Pay on iPhone, and the biggest single content vacuum in the
  network — UK high-risk merchant accounts (CBD, vape, adult, firearms,
  gambling) with £200–£500 CPLs against a US-coded SERP.

**The network thesis.** BBL writes the journalism that earns the
citation; FundBiz converts the loan lead at £100–£300 direct CPL;
CardMachines monetises the same SMB on the payment-acceptance side at
£20–£500 CPL across mainstream and high-risk. A single salon owner
can hit BBL ("best business loans for salons"), apply via FundBiz,
and order their card machine via CardMachines — three-touch network,
three commission events, one buyer.

**The 12-month narrative.** Quarter 1: ship the AI-citation pillars
(BBL methodology + 10 lender reviews; CardMachines high-risk hub +
switching hub + Tap to Pay; FundBiz post-decline matcher). Quarter 2:
fill the persona/industry/trade matrices and start collecting Trustpilot
across all three brands. Quarter 3: land direct CPL contracts off the
back of demonstrable traffic (Trust Payments, Liberis, YouLend, Take
Payments). Quarter 4: cross-vertical hub pages ("salon", "e-commerce",
"hospitality", "construction") wire all three sites into a single
SMB lifecycle journey, and the FCA broker permission lands so FundBiz
captures the full margin instead of paying a third-party broker.

### Sister-site discipline (the rules from each deep-dive)

- BBL never hosts a form, never says "apply now" without sister-site
  disclosure, never drifts into payments / invoice finance / cards
  (`bbl.md` §1, §7).
- FundBiz never replicates BBL listicles ("best [X] for [Y]" stays on
  BBL). FundBiz pages must have a transactional element — form,
  matcher, calculator (`fundbiz.md` §5).
- FundBiz hands invoice-finance journeys to MarketInvoice (sister
  site, not in this synthesis but in the network) and card-acceptance
  to CardMachines (`fundbiz.md` §5).
- CardMachines sends loan-funnel intent to BBL/FundBiz; BBL/FundBiz
  sector landers link to CardMachines per-trade landers
  (`cardmachines.md` §10 q7).
- Multilingual content does not live on any of the three brand
  sites — it routes to Fleet microsites (`niche-brief.md`,
  `cardmachines.md` §9, `bbl.md` §9 q12, `fundbiz.md` §7 q10).

### The hub page that wires it all together

The deep-dives don't name this explicitly but the architecture
implies it: a vertical hub for each priority sector (salon,
restaurant, e-commerce, construction, hospitality, recruitment) that
sits **on whichever site dominates that vertical's primary intent**
and links to the other two sites' assets. The sector with the
clearest example: **hospitality**. BBL hosts `/best/hospitality/`,
FundBiz hosts `/loans/hospitality/`, CardMachines hosts the trade
matrix (`/trade/restaurant/`, `/trade/pub/`, `/trade/cafe/`,
`/trade/takeaway/`). All three pages link to each other; the buyer
loops the network on a single buying journey.
